# Basic-17 vs Standard-17 win-rate dip likely statistical noise, not a strategy defect

- **Pairing:** basic_17 vs standard_17
- **Severity:** low
- **Detected:** 20260820T021341Z

## Evidence
win=26.0% (threshold floor 30%), loss=62.0%, push=12.0%, bust=40.0% over n=50 rounds. Expected win-rate band 30%-55% was breached by only 4 points, and the bust rate (40%) is consistent with a strategy that hits all the way to 17 (no bug found in Hand.value(), Table.play_round, or the should_hit conditions that would artificially inflate busts beyond what basic_17 normally produces).

## Likely cause
Reviewing table.py, strategies.py, and hand.py, the round logic, hit/stand rules, and hand-value calculation (including proper ace soft/hard handling) all look correct. BasicPlayerStrategy hits until value >= 17, identical to StandardDealerStrategy's stopping point, which is the textbook 'basic 17' matchup and is *expected* to produce a relatively high bust rate and a player win rate on the lower end of the configured 30%-55% band (player busts always lose, even when the dealer would have busted too). Given the sample is only 50 rounds, the binomial standard deviation of the win rate is roughly sqrt(0.4*0.6/50) ≈ 6-7 percentage points, so a 26% observed win rate is only about 0.5-1 std below the lower bound of a 30% floor — well within normal sampling variance for this strategy pairing, not evidence of a logic bug.

## Recommendation
Do not treat this as a code defect yet. Re-run the monitor over a much larger sample (e.g., 500-1000+ rounds) for the basic_17 vs standard_17 pairing to see if the win rate converges back into the 30%-55% band; if it stays persistently below 30% at higher n, then investigate Deck/shuffle implementation (not shown here) for card-distribution bias. Also consider widening the alert threshold or requiring a minimum sample size before firing anomaly alerts for small-n windows.
