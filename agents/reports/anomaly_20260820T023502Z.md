# Elevated player bust rate due to over-aggressive hit threshold in aggressive_20 strategy

- **Pairing:** aggressive_20 vs standard_17
- **Severity:** low
- **Detected:** 20260820T023502Z

## Evidence
Player bust rate 68.0% (ceiling 40%) and player win rate 20.0% (expected range 30-55%) both breached over 50 rounds, loss rate 76.0%, push 4.0%. Root code: AggressivePlayerStrategy.should_hit returns hand.value() < 20, compared to StandardDealerStrategy/BasicPlayerStrategy's < 17 threshold - a 3-point higher stopping point that statistically drives excess busts.

## Likely cause
AggressivePlayerStrategy.should_hit() in casino/strategies.py hits on any hand value < 20, versus the standard strategy's threshold of 17. This is by design (per the class docstring, it's a deliberate stress-test fixture), but it mathematically forces the player to keep drawing on hands of 17, 18, and 19 - values that are already strong in blackjack and carry substantial bust risk with each additional card. Since the strategy never stands until it hits 20+, it draws more cards on average than a standard strategy, sharply increasing the probability of exceeding 21. This directly explains the observed 68% bust rate and the correspondingly low 20% win rate (busted hands auto-lose per Table._outcome / play_round logic, regardless of the dealer's subsequent outcome).

## Recommendation
Confirm this is expected/intentional test behavior: aggressive_20 is documented as a deliberate stress-test fixture meant to produce anomalous stats, so no code fix is likely needed. If this pairing is appearing in production monitoring unexpectedly, verify why aggressive_20 is being invoked outside of test contexts (e.g., check strategy selection/config logic in whatever caller assigns player_strategy to Table), and consider excluding known stress-test strategy names from live anomaly alerting or tagging their alerts as expected/suppressed.
