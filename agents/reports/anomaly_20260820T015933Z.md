# Basic player strategy (basic_17) underperforming vs standard_17 dealer — win rate below threshold

- **Pairing:** basic_17 vs standard_17
- **Severity:** medium
- **Detected:** 20260820T015933Z

## Evidence
Over 50 rounds: win=24.0% (below expected 30%-55% range), loss=54.0%, push=22.0%, bust=34.0%. Bust rate of 34% is unusually high for a 17-stand policy and directly explains the loss/win skew given _outcome() awards the round to the dealer immediately when the player busts, without the dealer needing to act or bust themselves.

## Likely cause
BasicPlayerStrategy.should_hit() in casino/strategies.py takes dealer_upcard as a parameter but never uses it — it hits on every hand valued below 17 regardless of what the dealer is showing: `return hand.value() < 17`. Real basic strategy would stand on many hard totals (e.g., 12-16) when the dealer's upcard is weak (2-6), since the dealer is likely to bust on their own. By ignoring dealer_upcard entirely, this implementation hits far more often than true basic strategy in exactly the situations where standing is correct, which mechanically inflates the player's bust rate. The observed 34% player bust rate is high, and since play_round() awards the round to the dealer immediately on player bust (before the dealer even has to act), every one of those busts is an automatic loss — dragging win% down and loss% up. This is a structural characteristic of the simplified strategy, not a bug in Table/Hand/Deck.

## Recommendation
1) Re-run with a larger sample (e.g., 5,000+ rounds) to rule out variance — 50 rounds is a small sample and a single-digit swing in outcomes can move win rate several points. 2) If the low win rate persists at scale, treat this as expected behavior of BasicPlayerStrategy (it is a simplified/naive 'hit until 17' policy, not true basic strategy) and either (a) widen the expected win-rate band for basic_17 specifically, or (b) implement a dealer-upcard-aware strategy (e.g., stand on hard 12-16 vs dealer 2-6) and compare its bust/win rates against this baseline to confirm the hypothesis.
